THE GAME

You are Wick, a lamplighter moth carrying the last lantern across an island at the last minute of sunset. The lantern aims and fires by itself; you only move, and every kill feeds it light. Each time it fills, the game’s Settings menu opens and you sell a piece of the game for power: the subtitles, the HUD, the difficulty, your own Up key, and at the end of the night, the screen itself, one pixel at a time.

THE THEME

In this game, the trade-off is the settings menu. Every graphics and audio setting trades quality for performance. Luxel asks you to trade the game itself for yours.

You only move. The lantern aims at the nearest enemy and fires by itself, so your one job in play is where Wick stands.

THE LOOP
  1. Move. Snuffers walk in from the dark and the lantern shoots them.
  2. Each kill bursts into light shards. The lantern drinks them one by one, and each chime lands a step higher up the scale as it fills.
  3. When the lantern is full, it rings, the world freezes and the Settings menu opens.
  4. Sell one setting for a power, or pick BACK to keep everything and heal a heart. A gold NEW: tag on a row means that sale lights a new weapon for the lantern.
  5. The run resumes. The dark gets busier, new tabs open, and the next menu asks for something you’ll miss more.

TIPS FOR JUDGES
  • In a browser, click or tap the game once before you start. That gives it focus and lets the sound play. On a phone, turn it sideways first.
  • The first play opens with a 24-second story intro (any key skips it) and a tutorial of about half a minute in which Wick can’t be hurt. Both play once. Later runs start straight away.
  • Sell at every menu. The powers are what keep you alive: a player who never sells falls before act 3.
  • Every level-up heals one heart until you sell HEALING.
  • From level 12 (act 3, when CONTROLS opens) a hit on your last heart still can’t end the run. Instead THE LANTERN SPILLS: the gauge empties and its shards scatter around Wick. You have 4 seconds to run them back in, then they’re gone. The results card counts SAVED BY THE LANTERN ×N. Get that far and you will see the ending.
  • Catch the Magpie. Killing it drops its star into the lantern for a free level-up.
  • Dying is cheap. Restarting from the death card puts you back in place, with no intro or tutorial, and the first menu comes again at about 0:20.
  • The ending runs about a minute. Press twice to skip to the results.
  • If the game loses focus, it pauses.

THE SETTINGS SHOP

The upgrade shop is the game’s own Settings menu. Every level-up opens it with the world frozen, and you sell one step of one setting for a power. You get stronger, and the game gets harder to play.

TAP A TAB OR A ROW (OR USE ARROWS AND SPACE). NOTHING HERE IS REALLY SOLD. FOR THAT, TRY SETTINGS AT THE BOTTOM RIGHT.

THE RULES
  • One sale per menu. Or BACK: keep everything and heal one heart. Once HEALING is sold, BACK reads KEEP ALL and heals nothing.
  • Tabs open as you level up: GENERAL and GRAPHICS at level 2, INTERFACE at 4, GAMEPLAY at 8, CONTROLS at 12. A gold badge on a tab counts the rows you can sell there right now.
  • From level 12 a hit on your last heart can’t end the run. THE LANTERN SPILLS: the gauge empties and its shards scatter around Wick. Run them back in within 4 seconds or they’re gone. The results card counts SAVED BY THE LANTERN ×N.
  • Act 1 sells your comfort, act 2 your safety, act 3 your hands.
  • Powers stack. Damage, fire rate, speed and range multiply; hearts, shots and pierce add up.
  • The beauty rule: anything that changes how the island looks, how the music sounds or how the game feels is stamped NOT FOR SALE, with a padlock and the word LATER. For the first four and a half minutes the island and the music stay beautiful. The AUDIO and GRAPHICS tabs sit there, locked, from the second menu on.

BUY BACK

Once you have sold 11 settings, the menu offers one way back. Once per run, instead of selling, you can buy one sold setting back: it returns one step, and you keep the power it paid. It costs you that menu’s sale, and the row closes for good.

  • The menu’s header turns gold: SELL ONE OR BUY ONE BACK ONCE.
  • Rows you can return show BUY BACK in gold. With the cursor on one, the quip reads COSTS THIS LEVEL. YOU KEEP THE POWER.
  • 14 settings can come back: TIPS, SCREEN SHAKE, TIMER, LANTERN GAUGE, HEARTS, WARNINGS, DIFFICULTY, MERCY TIME, KNOCKBACK, HEALING, MOVEMENT, INPUT DELAY, AIM ASSIST and UP KEY. SUBTITLES and RAY TRACING have nothing to return, and LANGUAGE wears off by itself.
  • After the purchase: BOUGHT BACK. POWER KEPT. The light streams out of the lantern instead of in, and the restored setting shows over Wick in gold, for example MOVEMENT: SNAPPY.

WHY IT ENDS THIS WAY

The story’s premise is that DISPLAY: OFF is the sacrifice and the win at once. The dark swallows the last light, but the lantern kept every setting you sold, a whole game’s worth of light, more than the dark can eat.

WORLD AND CAST

At sunset the dark comes to eat the island’s light, and every night Wick the lamplighter carries one lantern out and lights it all again. Tonight the dark is hungrier, so the lantern takes the only light it has left to reach: the game you are playing.

There is no lore beyond that. The trade is with the lantern: it buys settings and pays in power. The enemies are the dark. They ate light, so a kill gives light back.

WICK

A lamplighter moth with a wine wing-cloak marked with sunset eye-spots, an ice ruff and leaf antennae. Gold and cream show on Wick only where the lantern lights them. About 24 pixels tall. Wick never talks.

  • Drawn in three views (side, front, back), and every move plays a hop.
  • 93 sprite sheets: the body, the lantern at three glow tiers, the shadow and the hit flash.
  • One-shot animations carry the story: the spawn drop, the cast, the level-up raise, the sell, and the ending’s sit-down with the lantern in Wick’s lap.

LIGHT SHARDS

Every kill drops light shards. They pop out, hover a moment, then the lantern drinks them one by one along a curve. There is nothing to walk over and pick up. Each arrival is timed to land on a 16th note of the music, and its chime climbs the scale as the lantern fills, from a low D to a high D. The shard that fills it sets off a chord that wants to resolve, and the menu’s sting resolves it.

THE DARK

Six enemies, and each asks for a different way of moving: mow, sidestep, chase, flank, weave, and keep close to the light. Each one’s warning lives in a setting you can sell, so every sale is a real trade against what is on the field.

They share a family look: cool violet bodies, never gold or cream (that is Wick’s light), and a pair of pale eyes as the brightest part. In the finale they are reduced to just those eyes.

THE LAST LIGHT LOOK

Every pixel in Luxel is one of 20 colours, lit by one low sun in the west. The look is called Last Light: an island garden in a starry void at the last minute of sunset. Hushed, golden, a little melancholy.

THE RULES
  • 320x180 pixels, scaled by whole numbers with nearest filtering, so every pixel stays square.
  • Top-down three-quarter view, like A Link to the Past or Eastward. Characters are about 24 pixels tall.
  • One sun in the west-north-west. Lit faces are left and top. Shadows fall long to the lower right, about 0.8 times the height of what casts them, one palette step darker, with a dithered tail.
  • Warm west, cool east: gold rims and flecks in the west, sky and ice highlights past the river.
  • Lit-side outlines. The shadow side gets the darkest night colour; the sunlit side gets one shade darker than the pixel it touches. No ink-black lines anywhere.
  • Glows are painted with palette colours and one 4x4 ordered dither, anchored to the world so neighbouring pieces line up. Nothing is blended or additive.
  • No text in the world, no neutral greys and no pure black. The darkest colour, void, belongs to the sky.

ALL SETTINGS: HIGHSOLD
BEAUTIFUL, THEN BROKEN

The whole game is about selling the way it looks, so the art has two jobs at once. The world has to be gorgeous at full settings, so every sale visibly costs something, and still readable when it is degraded: 4 colours, 2 colours, half brightness, 2 to 4 pixel blocks.

  • Values are planned for the worst case: walkable ground is mid-dark, paths mid, props light on top and dark at the base. Every zone has one landmark you can read in two colours at 4-pixel blocks.
  • Anything that gives off light also draws itself switched off, so BRIGHTNESS can swap in the dark version.
  • Each zone stars a setting you can sell: lights, particles, animation, stained-glass colour, fine detail, the stars.
  • Enemies stay readable in every mode. At 2 colours their eyes and one light edge survive; at 4-pixel blocks the silhouette alone says which enemy it is.
  • ANIMATIONS freezes every sprite on frame 1, so frame 1 of every loop is a good still pose, and frame 1 of every warning animation already shows the warning.
THE HOP RULE

Anything that moves in four directions has side, front and back frames, and every move plays a hop: crouch, air, land. A side view alone looks wrong on up and down moves. Secondary parts, like Wick’s antennae and hem, lag one frame behind the body. The Parasol Toad goes further, with eight facings, because which way its parasol points is the whole mechanic.

MOTION

The world loops on a 2-second cycle at 12 frames a second, and every frame count divides 24. Motion is subtle and nothing flashes. Screen shake is only for taking damage, and you can sell that too.

THE TITLE

LUXEL is written in luxels: each lit pixel of the 3x5 font is a 4-pixel lamp on a night grid. On the title screen they light west to east with a lantern note each, and their light spills out over the grove. When you press a key, the dark eats the title east to west in 0.6 seconds, the intro’s lights-out in small. At the end of the ending, the same luxels fly out of Wick’s lantern and build the name again.

AUDIOD HARMONIC MINOR. 144 BPM.

The soundtrack is Lantern Gothic: a galloping minor hook on a square lead over harpsichord, a pumping bass, organ on the backbeat and a tower bell on the phrase tops, in D harmonic minor at 144 BPM. In the game it follows the run’s clock phrase by phrase.

MUSIC THAT WAITS FOR YOU

Menus freeze the world, but the music keeps playing behind the menu’s wall, muffled. It never runs ahead of you: the score waits at the end of a phrase until the run catches up, so night falls when the run reaches night, however long you spend in the menus.

THE SETTINGS ARE PART OF THE SCORE
  • The menu wall: while a menu is open the run track sits behind it, low-passed at 500 Hz and 8 dB down.
  • Selling MUSIC winds the band down like a tape in 0.45 seconds, under an organ sigh and one low bell.
  • AUDIO QUALITY drops everything through a 700 Hz filter and a lo-fi crush: a phone call from down a well.
  • SOUND sold: the coins of that sale are the last sound you hear.
  • The lantern notes and the ending theme always play, whatever you have sold.
LIGHT SHARDS: KILLS CLIMBING TWO LEVELS OVER THE RUN LOOP.
Shard pickups are tuned chimes and bells on the 16th-note grid in D harmonic minor; when the gauge fills, an organ swell plays the full lantern.
THE SHARDS SING

Each light shard lands in the lantern on a 16th note of the run track. Its glass chime climbs D harmonic minor from D5 to D7 as the lantern fills, so the sound alone tells you how full it is. The shard that fills it lands on the top D and sets off a rolled A major chord, the dominant, and the level-up sting resolves it. The design doc names the models: Thresh’s soul pull, and Peggle’s rising pitch. When the arc changes key, the shard chimes follow it: E becomes E flat for Night, and F and B flat become F sharp and B for Last light.

THE LANTERN NOTES

The lantern has its own voice: ten recorded notes from A4 to D6. They play the 100 notes of the PIXELS sale, one per pixel, over a drone. Mashing Space to fix the screen climbs D harmonic minor from A4, one note a press. When the island comes back in the ending, every tenth PIXELS note plays in reverse, like a rewind. On the title screen each luxel plays a quiet lantern note as it lights.

  2. IDEA

PRESS KIT

ONE LINE

A survivor game where the upgrade shop is the Settings menu. Sell the game itself for power.

SHORT DESCRIPTION

You are Wick, a lamplighter moth with the last lantern. Every level-up opens the game’s Settings menu: sell the subtitles, your hearts, your Up key for power. At the end of the night the menu sells the rest itself, down to the last pixel.

LONG DESCRIPTION

Luxel is a short survivor game about the trade-off in every settings menu. You are Wick, a lamplighter moth carrying the last lantern across an island at the last minute of sunset. You only move. The lantern aims and fires by itself, and every kill sends light back into it.

When the lantern fills, the game’s own Settings menu opens, and that menu is the shop. Sell the subtitles for fire rate, the HUD for damage, the difficulty, your healing, your grip on the ground, your own Up key. Each sale makes you stronger and the game harder to play, and the best-looking settings stay locked away until the end of the night.

Then the dark comes all at once, and the menu starts selling the game by itself: the music, the colours, the frame rate, the lights. The last setting is the screen, and you sell it one pixel at a time. A run takes about six and a half minutes. Made in Godot for a game jam with the theme Trade-off.

FEATURES
  • An upgrade shop that is the game’s own Settings menu, with 19 sales to choose and a quip for every one
  • Arrows or WASD to move, or drag a thumb on a phone; the lantern aims and fires by itself
  • A pixel island in 20 colours, drawn entirely in code, that loses its looks one setting at a time
  • An original soundtrack in D harmonic minor that follows your run phrase by phrase
  • A finale where the menu sells the game by itself, down to DISPLAY: OFF, and a cinematic ending
